## Fraud problem and sources

The pipeline brings together activation, billing, usage, bundle, and subscriber data to identify SIM swap takeover, international revenue share fraud (IRSF), subscription fraud, and bundle abuse.

The first demo uses:
1. SIM activation events
2. Revenue files (currently prepaid)
3. Voice and data usage
5. Bundle purchases
8. Subscriber profile reference data

Bundle catalog is reference data. Bundle usage (source 6) and churn/customer state (source 7) are deferred. Repeated purchases are only a proxy for bundle abuse; do not claim to detect over-consumption without bundle-usage data.

- Athena setup refreshes external table metadata and re-registers partitions; it does not delete the S3 Parquet data.

The `make week4` target runs Gold, refreshes Athena tables and partitions, and writes rule candidates. LocalStack must be running. First-time Athena startup may download a large Trino engine and take several minutes.

## Known limitations to preserve

- All records and labels are simulated; no real telecom data is used.
- Revenue currently covers prepaid only; postpaid is not yet represented in the runner.
- The repeat-purchase rule is a proxy, not actual bundle-consumption abuse detection.
- Rules create candidate alerts; they do not block accounts or publish SNS notifications.
- The rules are heuristics, not production-calibrated policies.
- `simulator/run.py` may not call `writers.write_all()` even though some documentation/targets imply AWS writes; inspect before claiming that `make simulate` reproduces all AWS writes.
- Don’t overwrite the user’s existing changes to `simulator/config.yaml`.
